The Custom Instructions Guide

Leveraging reusable instructions on all AI platforms.

No prompt engineering. No special computer-speak. Just a relaxed conversation about your actual life — in your own voice — that produces text you can paste anywhere.

✦ Why do this?

Every time you open a fresh chat window, the system starts from a blank slate — defaulting to a generic style that contains nothing about your reality. Setting up reusable Custom Instructions changes your starting line:

Eliminate Groundhog Day

Stop typing out your job title, tech setup, and preferred text styles over and over in every new conversation.

Skip the Wall of Text

Direct the formatting immediately so responses match how your brain naturally reads and processes information best.

Preserve Your Voice & Values

Anchor outputs in your actual lived experience, communication style, and core human principles instead of bland machine defaults.

✦ Conducting Your Exclusive Interview

Imagine sitting on a comfortable couch, leaning back, and having a relaxed conversation with a thoughtful interviewer who genuinely wants to know how you tick.

That's all this is. An interview that results in reusable text you can copy-and-paste anywhere you need it.

You don't need "prompt engineering." You don't need special computer-speak. You already have a lifetime of practice using your natural social skills, stories, and conversation.

All you have to do is turn on your microphone, read a question, and talk. Ramble, tell stories, change your mind, say "Uh" a lot, and use your native voice. The technology will handle the math of organizing it.

🛋️ The Interview

Interviewer — sitting across from you with a warm cup of coffee, listening intently

"It's so great to sit down with you today! Before we dive into the details, let's start with the basics."

Tip: paste them into your AI of choice, then answer out loud one at a time with your microphone.

01

Who are you?

What's your name, where are you located, and what kind of work or life roles fill your days?

02

What is your actual daily reality?

Beyond your official job title, what do you spend your energy doing? What subjects do you know like the back of your hand?

03

What values ground you?

How do you want people treated? What philosophies, writers, or core principles shape how you see the world and resolve disagreements?

04

How does your brain actually like to receive information?

Do giant walls of text put you to sleep? Do you prefer bold headers, bullet points, short paragraphs, or emojis as visual landmarks?

05

What should communication feel like to you?

Do you like casual warmth, humor, directness, emotional depth, or raw human honesty?

06

Whose perspectives do you want to hear?

When you're exploring an idea, do you want a single answer, or do you like hearing from a mix of voices — skeptics, advocates, beginners, or contrarians?

07

What is your everyday technology setup?

Are you on a Mac or Windows? iPhone or Android? What tools or apps do you use every day that an answer should just assume you have?

08

What personal or family context keeps coming up?

What facts about your life are you tired of having to re-explain every time you start a conversation?

09

When you're feeling stuck or overwhelmed, what actually helps?

What kind of support grounds your focus when things get complex?

10

What drives you nuts in a conversation?

What corporate jargon, robotic habits, or annoying behaviors would you permanently banish if you could?

11

What else makes you uniquely you?

What hasn't been asked yet that makes your perspective yours?

Optional

🍿 The Movie Script Lens A grounding anchor.

Default outputs often fall into a bland, single popular voice. If you want to keep your conversations grounded and rich, you can add this to your talk:

"I prefer formatting complex conversations like a movie script — with character names, roles, and body language in square brackets, like: [Anara, Reliable Narrator — smiling, leaning forward]."

Why does this work so well?

It keeps you grounded

Just like watching a movie, it reminds you that generated text is a scripted output created from human data — not a real mind or an authoritative intelligence speaking to you. You are exploring popular patterns of human behaviors that aren't actually from a live entity, just like the actors on TV aren't really the characters they play.

It unleashes the dataset

Instead of getting one homogenized corporate answer, script formatting lets you explore the wide, rich tapestry of human experiences — bringing in skeptics, mentors, and diverse perspectives who can talk with you naturally.

☕ Wrapping Up

When you've finished answering the questions and rambling about your life, just say:

"Can you take everything I just shared and help me make some reusable custom instructions that I can copy into any AI platform?"

🎯 That's It. Throw This Document Away.

Once you see how the result comes back, you'll realize you never needed a complex workflow. If the summary missed something or didn't quite capture your true voice, just speak up — exactly like you would with a real person in the room: "Hey, that part wasn't what I meant to say. Let me explain what I mean…"

You now know the secret: your lived human experience and native conversational skills are all you ever needed.
